Hear your name in star language

Every letter is paired with a real pulsing star, and each star plays its own pulse. Type a name, in English or Arabic, and listen.

The letter-to-star pairing is a game. Each star’s note is real, played at its exact pitch. The small number is how far that note sits from the nearest piano note, in cents (a cent is a hundredth of a piano step).

About this sky

What is real

These 139 stars really exist. Their positions, distances, colours and pulses were measured by two space telescopes. A star’s note is its own pulse, sped up until we can hear it. Two stars that pulse at different speeds really do differ in pitch by the same musical gap.

What is a game

Pairing letters with stars is just for fun, and so is the echo, which grows with distance. Note names like F♯ depend on a human convention for the pitch of A. The soft hum in the background is atmosphere and never changes a star’s note.

Who measured these stars

Gaia is a European space telescope that maps the Milky Way. It measured where these stars are, how far away, and what colour. TESS is a NASA space telescope that watches stars brighten and dim, mainly to find planets. It recorded these pulses. SIMBAD and the GCVS are star catalogues that astronomers share. They supplied the official names.

Honest limits

Real brightness here is a rough estimate, because dust that dims starlight is not corrected and distances have errors. Gaia’s temperature guesses are too hot for many of these stars, so the cards say so.
